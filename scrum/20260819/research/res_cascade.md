# Literature sweep: small-model cascades, routing, and LLM-as-filter architectures for Parcel

Angle: what published cascade/routing/judge/guard-model work says about the drafted three-band significance policy (always-forward / never-forward / Gemma judge band) and about the reliability limits of a local 4–12B judge scoring state-delta buffers.

---

## Key works

### 1. FrugalGPT — Chen, Zaharia, Zou (Stanford, 2023)
**What it does.** The founding LLM-cascade paper. Queries are sent to a sequence of increasingly expensive models; a learned *scoring function* (a small DistilBERT-class regressor, not an LLM) decides whether the cheap model's answer is reliable enough to return or must be escalated. Matches GPT-4 quality at up to 98% cost reduction on classification-shaped tasks. Key structural point: the reliability gate is a tiny discriminative model, cheaper and better-calibrated than asking a generative model to grade itself.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** The whisperer is exactly a FrugalGPT-shaped component: a cheap local stage deciding what deserves the expensive hosted model's attention. FrugalGPT's lesson is that the gate should be the *smallest thing that can make the decision* — a scorer, not a reasoner. Your judge band asks Gemma to "score this JSON," which is closer to the right shape than free-form reasoning, but the paper suggests going further: the salience decision over state-deltas is a classification problem, and a generative 4–12B model is arguably over-powered *and* under-calibrated for it.
**Design implications.** Frame the judge band's output as a small closed decision (forward / hold / merge-into-next), not an open numeric score. Long-term, log every (buffer, decision) pair so a distilled classifier can replace the generative judge — the buffers plus Qwen3-32B AutoRater labels are a free training set.

### 2. RouteLLM — Ong et al. (UC Berkeley / LMSYS, 2024, ICLR 2025)
**What it does.** Trains routers on Chatbot Arena preference data to send each query to a strong or weak model; >2× cost reduction with no quality loss, and — the important result — routers *generalize to model pairs they were not trained on*, because they learn query difficulty, not model identity.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** Two lessons. First, routing decisions transfer: a significance policy tuned against Qwen3-32B labels should keep working if the hosted model changes (mini → larger realtime model), so investing in the judge band is not throwaway. Second, RouteLLM's routers are trained on *outcome data*, not hand-written rubrics. Parcel already has the raw material — 25 captured conversation threads in `evals/companion/realtime_convo_v1/fixtures/`, the mission timeline in `proof_final.txt`, and full state snapshots — to build a labeled "did this update matter to the conversation" dataset instead of guessing thresholds.
**Design implications.** Treat the initial hand-written judge rubric as a bootstrap; plan a calibration loop where the AutoRater (Qwen3-32B in `evals/autorater/`) labels replayed buffers and Gemma's agreement is measured with Cohen's kappa, not percent agreement.

### 3. AutoMix — Aggarwal, Madaan et al. (NeurIPS 2024)
**What it does.** Small model answers, then *self-verifies* with few-shot prompting; a POMDP router uses that noisy self-verification signal to decide escalation to the large model. Improves cost-quality frontier by up to 89%. Crucially, it treats small-model self-assessment as a *noisy observation* requiring a decision layer on top — raw self-verification is too unreliable to act on directly.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** The judge band's Gemma verdict should be treated the same way: a noisy signal consumed by deterministic policy, never a direct actuator of the forward decision. The drafted design already puts rate caps and dedup outside the judge — AutoMix says extend that: thresholds, hysteresis, and defaults on top of the judge score should also be deterministic and tuned against measured noise.
**Design implications.** Give the judge band explicit uncertainty handling: high-confidence forward, high-confidence drop, and a *middle band default*. Because a forwarded system item costs ~fractions of a cent (observed sessions ran $0.009–0.034 total against a $25/month budget) while a missed important update produces stale-state speech, the middle-band default should be **forward** — the rate cap, not the judge, is the budget guard. This is a Neyman-Pearson setup: fix the false-negative (missed-forward) rate, let dedup control volume.

### 4. Language Model Cascades: Token-level Uncertainty and Beyond — Gupta, Jitkrittum et al. (Google, 2024)
**What it does.** Studies *deferral rules* for generative cascades. Shows naive sequence-level confidence is length-biased (long outputs look uncertain, short ones overconfident) and that small learned post-hoc deferral rules over token-level uncertainty features beat simple aggregation.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** If the whisperer reads Gemma's confidence at all, read it at the *decision token* (the logit of the "forward"/"hold" token under constrained decoding), not over a generated rationale — rationale-length effects will corrupt any score aggregated over free text. This is the same trick ShieldGemma uses (below): one-token verdict, probability read from the logit.
**Design implications.** Constrain the judge to emit a single enum token first; log its probability; threshold on it. If a rationale is wanted for debugging, generate it *after* the verdict token so it cannot bias the score.

### 5. Judging the Judges — Thakur et al. (2024)
**What it does.** Benchmarks nine judge models against humans on TriviaQA — a deliberately *easy*, objective judging task. Llama-3 70B and GPT-4 Turbo reach Cohen's kappa ~0.79/0.84 (humans: 0.96). Llama-3 **8B reaches only kappa ~0.62 even though its raw percent agreement exceeds 80%** — judges with 80%+ agreement can still assign scores 20 points apart. Percent agreement is systematically misleading.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** This is the cleanest published answer to "how well does an 8B-class judge agree with a frontier judge": on the easiest possible task, agreement is moderate, not excellent, out of the box. A stock Gemma-12B scoring salience will disagree with Qwen3-32B a meaningful fraction of the time, and measuring that disagreement with percent agreement will hide it. Interestingly, the fine-tuned JudgeLM-7B *ranked systems* correctly despite worse absolute alignment — small judges are better at relative/thresholded decisions than absolute scores.
**Design implications.** (a) Validate Gemma-vs-Qwen with kappa and per-class recall (especially recall on "should-forward"); (b) prefer binary/ternary decisions over 1–10 scores — absolute scoring is where small judges are weakest; (c) do not skip the validation step because spot-checks "look agreeing."

### 6. JudgeBench — Tan et al. (ICLR 2025)
**What it does.** Converts hard reasoning datasets into judge tasks with objectively correct labels. Result: on genuinely hard verdicts, **even GPT-4o judges perform near random (≈50%)**; Claude-3.5-Sonnet reaches only 64%; only reasoning models (o3-mini class) reach ~81%. Judge reliability collapses when the verdict requires reasoning the judge cannot itself perform.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** This locates the complexity cliff. Small judges are usable when the task is *recognition against an explicit rubric* and collapse when the task requires *inference*. For the whisperer: "did battery state change class?" or "is this a new block class since the last forward?" is recognition — safe. "Would the owner find this conversationally relevant right now?" requires modeling the dialogue state — that is inference, and it is exactly where a 4–12B judge (and even frontier judges) will silently fail.
**Design implications.** Keep the judge's question context-free and rubric-shaped: input = accumulated delta buffer + class annotations + summary of what was last forwarded; question = "does this buffer contain a state change of a kind on this list that has not been forwarded?" Do **not** feed it the conversation history and ask about relevance-to-dialogue — that moves the task across the cliff.

### 7. Prometheus 2 (KAIST, 2024) + Atla Selene Mini (2025) — specialized small judges
**What they do.** Prometheus 2 (7B/8x7B) is fine-tuned specifically for evaluation with explicit scoring rubrics; it roughly halves the open-vs-GPT-4 correlation gap (e.g., FLASK human correlation 0.555 vs GPT-4's 0.679). Selene Mini (8B, Llama-3.1 base, SFT+DPO on curated critique data) outperforms GPT-4o-mini across 11 judge benchmarks and beats GPT-4o on RewardBench — an 8B model, runnable under llama.cpp/Ollama.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** Together with #5/#6 these bracket the answer to the focus question: *stock* small models are mediocre judges; *narrow-task-tuned* small models reach frontier-judge parity on rubric-driven evaluation. Salience-of-state-delta is far narrower than general evaluation, so a few-shot-prompted Gemma with a tight rubric should land somewhere between — and a lightly fine-tuned (or even LoRA'd) judge on a few hundred AutoRater-labeled buffers should be well inside the reliable zone. Both models also show *explicit rubrics in the prompt* are load-bearing: the rubric, not the model, carries most of the judgment.
**Design implications.** Write the significance rubric as an explicit enumerated artifact (versioned like the SI is in `prompting.py`), inject it into the judge prompt verbatim, and eval rubric versions with the existing AutoRater harness. Consider Selene-Mini itself as a drop-in judge baseline to benchmark Gemma against.

### 8. Llama Guard (Meta, 2023–24) & ShieldGemma (Google, 2024) — guard models as filters
**What they do.** Small classifier-shaped LLMs (1B–27B; ShieldGemma-2B is Gemma-2-based) that sit in front of / behind expensive models and emit constrained verdicts on a fixed policy taxonomy. ShieldGemma beats Llama Guard by ~10.8% AU-PRC and its verdict is read as the **probability of a single "Yes" token** — a calibrated continuous score from a generative model with zero parsing risk. These are the highest-volume production deployments of "small local LLM filters a stream for a big model."
**Why it matters for Parcel.** This is the production-proven template for the whisperer's judge band, on the same model family already running locally. The whisperer is structurally a guard model pointed at telemetry instead of user content: fixed taxonomy (your significance classes), constrained one-token verdict, logit-derived confidence, deterministic thresholds outside. Note ShieldGemma ships a *policy text* in every prompt — the taxonomy is prompt-injected, hence updatable without retraining, matching the versioned-rubric implication from #7.
**Design implications.** Implement the judge call as: rubric + buffer → single-token verdict via grammar, read the token logprob from llama.cpp (`logprobs` on the completion endpoint the reasoner already exposes), threshold deterministically. This eliminates most of the JSON-failure question by never asking for JSON at the decision point.

### 9. Outlines / grammar-constrained decoding (Willard & Louf 2023) + JSONSchemaBench (2025)
**What they do.** Outlines reformulates constrained generation as an FSM over the vocabulary — guaranteed-valid output with O(1) per-token overhead. JSONSchemaBench measures six engines (Guidance, Outlines, **llama.cpp**, XGrammar, OpenAI, Gemini) on ~10K real-world schemas: unconstrained generation has substantially lower compliance everywhere and drops hard on complex schemas; constrained decoding fixes syntax *and* improved downstream accuracy up to 4% while being ~50% faster (fewer wasted tokens). Coverage caveat: even the best engine only supports ~30–41% of *complex* real-world schemas (JSON Schema Store / GitHub-hard), ~96% of flat function-call-style schemas; llama.cpp's grammar engine works but had the worst measured per-token overhead (≈30ms TPOT vs Guidance 6.4ms in their setup).
**Why it matters for Parcel.** Direct answer to the focus question on format failure rates: an *unconstrained* llama.cpp Gemma on a score-this-JSON task will produce a non-trivial parse-failure rate (markdown fences, trailing prose, truncation — worse under quantization), and hoping otherwise contradicts every measurement. With a GBNF grammar the syntactic failure rate goes to ~zero, at some TPOT cost that is irrelevant at whisperer rates (≤1 Hz, tens of output tokens). Keep the schema flat (one enum + optional short string) and it sits in the ~96%-coverage regime.
**Design implications.** Ship the judge with a GBNF grammar in the llama.cpp request (the reasoner API at :8080 accepts `grammar`/`json_schema` on completions); still validate + retry-once as belt-and-suspenders (JSONSchemaBench explicitly recommends post-validation because engines under-constrain edge cases); on double failure fall back to a *deterministic* heuristic (e.g., forward iff the buffer contains a class not forwarded in N seconds) — judge failure must degrade to a rule, never to silence.

### 10. "Let Me Speak Freely?" (Tam et al., 2024) vs. "Say What You Mean" (dottxt, 2024)
**What they do.** Tam et al. showed strict format constraints can degrade reasoning performance, worst under the tightest constraints; the dottxt rebuttal showed the effect largely disappears with well-designed schemas and prompts that give the model room (e.g., a rationale field *before* the answer field, schema communicated in the prompt), and that constrained generation then matches or beats unconstrained.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** The judge band's task is shallow enough that constraint-induced reasoning loss should be minor — but the ordering lesson is free to adopt and the failure is silent if ignored: if the grammar forces `{"score": ...}` as the first tokens, the model must commit to the verdict before "thinking."
**Design implications.** If any structured output beyond one token is kept: schema order = short `reason` first, verdict last (or verdict-token-only per #8, with rationale generated afterward for logs only). Put the schema and an example in the prompt, don't rely on the grammar alone to communicate the format.

### 11. Apple Intelligence Foundation Models (2024–25) — production tiered inference
**What it does.** The most prominent shipped small-local + big-remote cascade: a ~3B on-device model handles the high-frequency, low-stakes work (summarization, notification *prioritization* — literally importance-weighting a stream of updates for the user); escalation to the server MoE happens only when the on-device model determines the task exceeds it, and the boundary is a product-legible event. Also ships constrained/guided generation as the *only* supported structured-output path for the on-device model.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** Independent production convergence on the drafted architecture: cheap local model as importance-filter over an event stream, expensive model touched sparsely, structured output only via constrained decoding at small scale. Notification prioritization is the closest shipped analog of the whisperer that exists. Also validates the asymmetry: Apple routes on *task class* first (deterministic) and model judgment second — like your always/never bands wrapping the judge band.
**Design implications.** Keep escalation observable (Parcel's panel/event ring already does this — every whisperer forward should be a visible event row, which the floor-gated narration channel gives you for free); treat "which band" as deterministic classification first, model judgment only inside the middle band.

### 12. Generative Agents — Park et al. (Stanford, 2023)
**What it does.** The canonical LLM-scored importance architecture: every observation in an agent's memory stream gets an LLM-assigned importance score (1–10, "mundane → poignant"), and retrieval combines **recency (exponential decay) × relevance (embedding similarity) × importance (LLM score)** as a weighted sum. Ablations showed pure-LLM or pure-recency scoring produced degenerate behavior; the *composite* is what worked.
**Why it matters for Parcel.** The judge band as drafted makes the LLM the whole significance function. Park et al.'s ablation is a warning: LLM importance alone is flat and noisy at the margins; it works when multiplied with cheap deterministic signals. Parcel already computes those signals — delta magnitude, block-class novelty (the mission_log edge-triggering from R4L is exactly a novelty detector), time-since-last-forward — they just aren't in the significance score.
**Design implications.** Make the forward decision `f(deterministic_novelty, time_decay, judge_score)` rather than `judge_score > τ`. The judge then only breaks ties on genuinely ambiguous buffers, shrinking both its call rate and the blast radius of its errors.

### 13. (Brief) Speculative execution in agent stacks — DSP, PASTE, SpecBox (2025–26)
**What they do.** Run a cheap approximation agent ahead of the expensive one; commit if a verifier accepts, roll back otherwise (Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning); speculatively pre-execute tools predicted from patterns (PASTE).
**Why it matters for Parcel.** Mostly *not* applicable to motion — speculation requires cheap rollback, and physical motion has none; Parcel's local-chain-commands-motion invariant is the correct anti-speculative stance. The applicable slice is conversational: the whisperer could pre-compose the candidate system item while the buffer accumulates, so a forward decision injects instantly rather than paying judge latency at decision time.
**Design implications.** Pipeline the judge: score buffer N while accumulating N+1; never speculate on anything that reaches the motion chain.

### Supporting data points on local-model instruction reliability
- **Gemma 3 technical report (2025):** IFEval — 4B-IT 90.2, 12B-IT 88.9, 27B-IT 90.4 (Gemma-2-27B-IT: 91.1). Instruction-following at 4B is now Gemma-2-27B class. **No JSON/function-calling evals are published in the report** — IFEval measures verifiable NL instructions, not schema compliance, so it upper-bounds but does not predict JSON reliability; quantized llama.cpp deployment degrades further (unmeasured in the report). Conclusion: do not extrapolate "IFEval 90" to "will emit clean JSON"; use grammar (#9).
- **Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard:** simple single-call AST accuracy for good small models is decent (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 ~85% on early BFCL simple-AST; fine-tuned 3B xLAM ~88% non-live AST) but **collapses to ~55% on multi-turn** even for tuned small models. The whisperer's one-shot classification is BFCL-simple-shaped (fine); anything multi-step delegated to local models is not.

## Design pressure

**What the drafted design gets right (per this literature):**
1. **Deterministic outer bands around a learned middle** is exactly the guard-model / Apple pattern and the cascade-deferral prescription: never let the model veto safety (always-forward band = the S13 "proximity STOP never withheld" invariant, already pinned by test), never spend model calls on per-tick noise.
2. **Rate caps and dedup outside the judge** — AutoMix/FrugalGPT both treat model verdicts as noisy signals under deterministic policy. R4L's live session 1 (69 narrations from one turn, 20/20 ring slots flooded in 2s) is the in-house proof that these must be mechanical.
3. **One system item via the floor-gated narration channel** reuses an existing, seeded, rate-limited delivery path rather than inventing a second mouth — consistent with the single-channel discipline the R4L card fought for.
4. **A local small model as filter for a hosted model** is production-validated (Apple, guard models) and cost-rational at Parcel's scale ($0.01–0.03/session observed vs $25/month budget).

**What it gets wrong or should change:**
1. **"Gemma scores accumulated buffers" as free-form numeric scoring is the weakest possible judge formulation.** Small judges are worst at absolute scores (kappa ~0.62 for 8B on even easy tasks; score clustering), best at constrained classification, and near-frontier only when rubric-driven. Reformulate: single-token enum verdict (forward/hold/merge) under GBNF grammar, confidence read from the verdict-token logprob, explicit versioned rubric in the prompt. This simultaneously answers the format-failure question — syntactic failure goes to ~zero — and removes retry complexity from the main path.
2. **LLM-only significance is under-specified.** Adopt the Generative-Agents composite: deterministic novelty (block-class edges you already compute) × time-decay × judge score. The judge should be a tie-breaker on the ambiguous middle, not the whole function.
3. **No calibration loop is specified.** The literature is unanimous that agreement must be *measured* (kappa, per-class recall), not assumed. Parcel has everything needed: replay `proof_final.txt`-style timelines and the 25 fixture threads through the buffer builder, label with the Qwen3-32B AutoRater, measure Gemma agreement. Set a floor (e.g., kappa ≥ 0.7 and should-forward recall ≥ 0.9) before the judge band is allowed to *suppress* anything.
4. **Missing middle-band default / cost asymmetry.** Forwarding is nearly free; a suppressed important update produces stale-state speech (the "I waved" over-claim class). Default ambiguous verdicts to forward; let dedup+rate-cap carry the budget. The judge exists to cut *volume*, not to be right about every item.

**Pitfalls the draft has not considered:**
1. **At-most-once delivery.** R4L/R5 both observed lane stalls swallowing turns/responses (`stalls: 2` in 2 minutes; a whole navigation turn unanswered and unbilled). An injected "significant update" can vanish silently. The whisperer needs delivery accounting: re-queue on stall/reconnect for always-forward items, and don't charge dropped items against the rate cap.
2. **Injected items become memory.** R5's cross-session contamination (memory-tail replay turned "wave at me" into a navigation) shows injected context steers the mini model hard and persists. Whisperer items must be formatted so they cannot be replayed as owner intent, and probably excluded from the memory tail — otherwise importance-weighted updates slowly poison future sessions.
3. **Weak instruction compliance of gpt-realtime-2.1-mini is a consumer-side constraint on the whisperer.** R5 proved the mini model violates SI rules it was explicitly given, live. So the whisperer cannot assume a forwarded item is spoken once, spoken correctly, or not spoken at all when marked FYI. Every forwarded item should carry an explicit speak/hold marker, and the AutoRater should score *what the model did with forwarded items* — this is a new eval class the drafted design doesn't mention.
4. **Judge-task scope creep across the complexity cliff.** The moment someone adds conversation history to the judge prompt to make it "relevance-aware," the task crosses from recognition (where 4–12B judges are fine) into inference (where JudgeBench shows even frontier judges hit chance). Guard the judge's input contract as strictly as the motion chain guards tool calls.
5. **Resource contention.** The judge shares the llama.cpp host with the Gemma reasoner (:8080); a scoring call queued behind a long planner generation adds seconds of latency at exactly the moment state is changing fast. Either a dedicated small judge instance (a 4B fits — IFEval says 4B ≈ old 27B) or strict low-frequency batching with a staleness bound on scored buffers.
6. **Scenarios 2–4 should not depend on the judge or the mini model at all.** The literature's routing lesson inverted: "ask vs act" (door → ask next; desk-sitting → refuse orbit; walking-not-running → ask) are precondition checks — recognition-shaped, deterministic-friendly, and `AskClarification` already sits in `admitted_skills`. Use local models to *detect* the mismatch (pace class, owner-seated) and let plan admission force the ask/refusal with template speech; trusting the weakly-compliant mini model to remember to ask is the one architecture the observed behavior in R4L/R5 already falsified.
