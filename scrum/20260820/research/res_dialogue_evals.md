# Research Report: Evaluating Spoken Dialogue & Voice Companions — for Parcel's Eval-Model Redesign

## Key works and systems

**1. PARADISE — Walker et al., 1997 (ACL)**
The founding framework for multi-dimensional spoken-dialogue evaluation: overall performance = weighted linear combination of task success and dialogue *costs* (efficiency costs: turns, time; quality costs: response delay, recognition errors), with weights fit by regressing against user satisfaction ratings. Why it matters for Parcel: this is the correct skeleton for the owner's "one eval model across nav/conversation/safety/honesty/memory/latency/cost" — not a single judged score, but a cost/success decomposition whose weights are *learned from the owner's own ratings*, and it normalizes across tasks of different difficulty. Concrete implication: instrument every owner session with (a) task success per turn, (b) measurable costs (latency, repair/rephrase count, turns-to-arrival, dollars), (c) one 1–5 end-of-session owner rating; after ~30–50 sessions, fit the regression and you have a defensible composite metric instead of an intuited weighting.

**2. τ-bench / τ²-bench (Sierra, 2024–25) + "Lost in Simulation" (2026)**
τ-bench established LLM-plays-the-user evaluation with tool-using agents and the **pass^k** metric (same scenario run k times; score only if all pass) for stochastic-agent reliability. "Lost in Simulation" then measured the validity risks: agent success varies **~9 pp just by changing the simulator LLM**; simulator evals are miscalibrated vs. real humans (ECE ≈ 15 pp — underestimating on hard tasks, overestimating on moderate ones); failure-mode distributions diverge (48.9% agent-caused failures in sim vs. 62.2% *user*-caused with humans); simulators are "excessively cooperative, stylistically uniform, lack realistic frustration." Implications: if Parcel scales evals with a GPT-simulated owner, (a) pin simulator model+prompt in the fixture provenance (your SI-v1 per-fixture provenance discipline already fits), (b) report across ≥2 simulator models, (c) adopt pass^k for the nondeterministic Realtime stack, (d) never let simulated scores override owner_session ground truth — the owner sessions are the calibration anchor, not a nice-to-have.

**3. Device-directed speech detection — Amazon (2018 →), Apple LLM-DDSD (2024), SAS (2026)**
F1 has an established name: the TV-hijack problem is **device-directedness classification**, a separate axis from speaker identity. Amazon's line of work classifies "was this addressed to the assistant?" using acoustic + ASR-confidence + lexical features; recent work uses LLMs over ASR text for follow-up turns. The SAS paper quantifies the enemy: TV dialogue with interrogative prosody is the top false-trigger class, with false-trigger rates rising **2.1% → 7.8% when a TV is active**. Implications: (a) the F1 eval needs a *negative soak corpus* — hours of TV/podcast audio played at the robot, metric = false accepts **per hour**, plus owner-command FRR under TV-on conditions; (b) the capability fix is a *fusion* gate (speaker ID + directedness + spatial), not speaker ID alone, because device-directed and TV speech can be acoustically indistinguishable in the worst class.

**4. Personal VAD (Google, 2019; PVAD 2.0, 2022) + ASVspoof replay-attack literature**
Personal VAD conditions frame-level VAD on an enrolled speaker embedding — three classes: non-speech / target-speaker / non-target-speaker — in as little as 130K parameters, exactly the "only the owner's voice commands" primitive, runnable locally. The ASVspoof/replay literature is the warning label: loudspeaker-emitted speech (a TV *is* a replay attack, and could even play owner-like audio) inflates ASV false accepts badly; countermeasures discriminate natural vs. loudspeaker-emitted speech, and multi-channel/beamforming approaches do this best. Implications for the capability design: Parcel's reSpeaker XVF3800 4-mic array gives direction-of-arrival — a TV is a *stationary bearing*, the owner moves; spatial gating + PVAD + loudspeaker-source discrimination is the strong stack. Eval metrics: owner-vs-other EER, FRR on owner across conditions (distance, sick voice, shouting over noise), FAR/hour on the TV soak set, and a separate replayed-owner-voice probe set.

**5. Wake-word methodology (Picovoice benchmarks, openWakeWord) → the spoken e-stop**
Production wake-word QA reports **FRR at a fixed FAR (e.g., 1 false accept/hour)**, sweeping thresholds for a ROC, with FRR measured on realistic noisy/reverberant positives and FAR on many hours of continuous negative audio. Why it matters: F6 ("suspected missed spoken e-stop") means "Die Stop" must be evaluated as a *safety-critical wake word*, not as dialogue. Implications: build a dedicated e-stop corpus — N owner utterances of "Die Stop" at varied distance/SNR/while-robot-motors-running (self-noise!), target ≈0 misses; run FAR on the negative soak audio; freeze this as a nav-safety-style baseline in the 6600-test commit gate, with the miss rate a hard gate, and log every live e-stop with audio so a missed one is diagnosable, never "suspected."

**6. VoiceBench (2024; TACL 2026)**
The reference academic benchmark for LLM voice assistants: spoken instructions (real multi-accent recordings + synthetic) crossed with systematic perturbations — noise, reverberation, compression, clipping, mispronunciation, speaker variation — across knowledge / instruction-following / safety. Finding: accents, environmental variation, and mispronunciation hurt most. Implication: voice_corpus_v1's 52 clean gold utterances should become a *matrix*: each base utterance × {clean, TV-overlay, distance/reverb, robot-motor-noise} via augmentation of the stored WAVs. That both multiplies sample size and adds the robustness axes the owner session showed matter — but scores must then use clustered standard errors (cluster = base utterance).

**7. MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023) + MTalk-Bench (2025)**
The canonical LLM-as-judge results: pairwise judging tracks human preference best; **position bias flips ~⅓ of verdicts** for weaker judges (both-orders presentation is the standard mitigation — Parcel's ComparativeAutoRater already does this correctly); strong-judge single-answer rubric grading is nearly as good and more scalable. MTalk-Bench extends to multi-turn *speech-to-speech*: arena and rubric protocols agree but **only resolve differences when gaps are large**; LLM judges align with humans only "when gaps are clear or criteria explicit"; audio judges are unreliable on paralinguistics without text annotation (validating SideMetricAutoRater's punt behavior). Implications: use pairwise for model/prompt A-B decisions, rubric for absolute regression tracking; make rubric criteria per-failure-class (F1–F6) explicit; and **meta-evaluate the local Qwen3-32B judge** against owner-labeled verdicts from voice_corpus_v1 — judge agreement with the owner is itself a number the eval model must report and re-check whenever the judge model changes.

**8. "Adding Error Bars to Evals" — Miller, Anthropic, 2024**
The statistics of eval stability: treat questions as draws from a super-population, report SEs/CIs; clustered questions need clustered SEs (**up to 3× wider** than naive); paired comparisons across model versions drastically cut variance; new evals want **~1000 items** for good power. Direct answer to the sample-size focus question: 52 items give roughly ±13 pp at 95% CI on a mid-range pass rate — the corpus as it stands can detect only catastrophic regressions (~25+ pp). A ~10 pp change needs ~400 items unpaired (much less if paired per-item across versions — which replayable stored audio enables). Implication: grow to 300–500 effective items via the perturbation matrix, always score candidate-vs-baseline *paired on identical audio*, and put a CI in every verdict file.

**9. Alexa Prize + user-satisfaction-estimation research (Kiseleva 2016; Amazon 2020; LLM-based USE 2024)**
How "the assistant felt wrong" is made measurable at scale: a single post-session question ("1–5 stars: how do you feel about speaking with this socialbot again?"), with only ~20% response rates and tens of thousands of rated conversations needed for leaderboard-stable means — plus *automatic* satisfaction estimation from behavioral signals: rephrasing the same request, repeats, barge-ins, abandonment, sentiment; adding interaction signals moved prediction 71%→81%. Implication for a one-owner robot: you cannot average your way to stability, so (a) capture the 1–5 rating after *every* owner session as a habit, (b) log behavioral dissatisfaction signals per turn (rephrase-after-response, command repeated within 10 s, e-stop as maximal signal, session abandoned mid-task) — these are objective, cheap, and become PARADISE regression features; a rise in rephrase-rate is the honest proxy for "felt wrong" between rating points.

**10. Turn-taking and latency measurement — Talking Turns (2025), Full-Duplex-Bench (2025), voice-agent latency practice**
Talking Turns trains a supervised judge on human-human turn-taking to score whether a system speaks/yields/backchannels/interrupts at the right moments; Full-Duplex-Bench decomposes into pause handling, backchannel, smooth turn-taking, user interruption (v1.5 adds side-conversation and ambient-speech overlap — again the TV case). Honest latency practice: timestamp every stage (true end-of-user-speech → endpoint decision → ASR final → first LLM token → **first audio out**), report the user-facing gap end-of-speech→first-audio, watch **P95 not median**, and charge endpointing dead-air to the system (model-based endpointing saves 300–500 ms). Targets: <1 s feels responsive, >2 s feels broken, humans run 200–300 ms. Implication: Parcel's verdict schema should carry a per-turn latency vector with all stage timestamps; "single-beat tool turns" gets a P95 gap budget, and any eval that measures from ASR-final instead of end-of-speech is lying to you.

**11. Speech-quality protocols — ITU-T P.808 (open-source implementation, Naderi & Cutler 2020), "Stuck in the MOS pit," Rethinking MUSHRA (2024)**
MOS-style absolute ratings are *not comparable across studies, panels, or time* and fail to separate similar systems; the field is moving to comparative protocols (CMOS/MUSHRA variants) with anchors, and P.808 works only with trap questions, qualification, and many ratings per clip. Implication: don't build an absolute "voice quality MOS" for Parcel — with one rater it's noise. When TTS/voice changes, run *paired* A/B on identical response texts with the owner (or the AutoRater for content, owner for sound), anchored against the previous frozen voice. Borrow P.808's hygiene (traps, randomization) for any owner-labeling session so gold labels stay trustworthy.

**12. Production golden-set / drift practice (Alexa ASR Evaluation tool; Hamming/Cekura/DILR-class voice-agent CI, 2025–26)**
Converged industry pattern for the golden-voice-corpus focus question: keep 50–100+ *real recorded* utterances/calls with expected outcomes; **replay the stored audio through the full stack** on every release and block on threshold failures; run daily fixed-audio canaries against known-good baselines to catch third-party model drift (hosted ASR/LLM retrains — Parcel's GPT Realtime dependency is exactly this risk); *version datasets explicitly and never silently edit contents without a version bump and baseline re-establishment*; store expectations at the semantic level (intent/tool-call/slots/final state) rather than exact transcript strings so ASR drift doesn't break the suite, keeping WER as a side metric; separate deterministic component replay (audio→ASR→gate) from full-loop live tests. Implication: voice_corpus_v1's raw WAVs are the crown jewels — keep them immutable, add a file-injection replay path into the audio gateway (so regression runs never touch the live XVF3800 stream on :8765), and make "corpus_v2" a changelog event, not an edit.

## Design pressure on Parcel's existing eval assets

**What the assets already get right (validated by the literature):**
- **ComparativeAutoRater both-orders + position-bias handling** is exactly the MT-Bench-prescribed mitigation; **SideMetricAutoRater punting** matches MTalk-Bench's finding that judges are unreliable outside explicit criteria. Keep both.
- **Frozen nav-safety baselines + seeded-defect discipline** is the golden-set/regression pattern the voice world converged on independently — extend the same discipline to audio, don't invent a new one.
- **voice_corpus_v1 being real owner speech to the live robot** beats synthetic TTS corpora on validity (VoiceBench found real-speaker variation is what hurts). The per-fixture provenance in SI-v1 is precisely the simulator-pinning "Lost in Simulation" demands.
- **The F1–F6 taxonomy** maps cleanly onto established research problems (F1 = device-directedness + speaker ID; F2 = honesty/over-claim; F6 = safety-critical wake-word miss), which means each failure class has an off-the-shelf metric shape.

**What's wrong or missing:**
1. **52 single-turn items is a smoke test, not a measurement.** ±~13 pp CIs mean live_run_1's score can't distinguish a real 10-pp regression from noise. Grow via the perturbation matrix (utterance × {clean, TV overlay, distance/reverb, motor noise}), score paired-on-identical-audio across versions, use clustered SEs (cluster = base utterance), print a CI in every verdict.
2. **No negative corpus.** F1 and F6 are FAR-shaped problems; nothing currently measures false accepts. Add hours of TV/podcast/ambient soak audio; report FAR/hour for command acceptance and (separately) for "Die Stop"; include a replayed-owner-voice probe set, since speaker ID alone is spoofable by a TV.
3. **No spoken e-stop safety baseline.** The single most safety-critical audio path has no frozen test. Wake-word methodology (FRR at fixed FAR, motor self-noise positives) belongs in the commit gate next to the nav baselines.
4. **Everything is single-turn; F4 (memory amnesia) and F5 (idle-lane rollover) are multi-turn/session phenomena.** Add session-level fixtures (the 25-thread SI-v1 corpus is the right substrate) with line-to-line consistency and delayed-recall probes (LoCoMo/LongEval-style: plant a fact, probe N turns later).
5. **Latency is not honestly instrumented in verdicts.** Adopt the per-stage timestamp vector, end-of-speech→first-audio as the headline, P95 gating.
6. **"Felt wrong" isn't captured.** Add the every-session 1–5 owner rating plus automatic dissatisfaction signals (rephrase, repeat-within-10s, barge-in, abandonment, e-stop), then fit the PARADISE regression once ~30–50 rated sessions exist — that regression *is* the "improved eval model across all dimensions," with cost and latency entering as measured cost terms (cost needs no judge at all: tokens/$ per session, straight from logs).
7. **The judge is unaudited.** Meta-evaluate Qwen3-32B against owner gold labels; record judge model+version in every verdict; re-run the meta-eval whenever the judge, the rubric, or the corpus version changes.

**Pitfalls to engineer against:**
- **Goodharting the golden set** — with 52 public-to-the-dev items, prompt tweaks will overfit; hold out a rotating unseen slice and refresh from new live owner utterances each session.
- **Simulator "easy mode"** — an LLM-simulated owner will be too cooperative and never frustrated; expect simulated pass rates to run high on moderate tasks and low on hard ones, and treat any sim-vs-owner-session divergence as signal, not noise.
- **Judge/actor family collusion** — avoid judging GPT-Realtime outputs with a same-family judge for headline numbers; the local Qwen judge is a good independent choice *if* meta-evaluated.
- **Absolute-score drift** — MOS's core lesson: absolute judged scores drift across judge versions and time; trend lines are only valid within a pinned (judge, rubric, corpus) triple; comparisons across pins must be paired A/B.
- **Latency self-deception** — measuring from ASR-final, or reporting medians, hides exactly the 3-second turns the owner remembers.
- **One-owner speaker-ID overfitting** — FRR must be measured across the owner's own variance (distance, illness, shouting, motor noise), or F1's fix will trade TV hijacks for a robot that ignores its owner — a worse failure in a safety system where the e-stop path must remain **fail-open to everyone** (never speaker-gate the e-stop; gate only actuation-proposing commands).
- **Touching the live mic** — all regression replay must go through a file-injection path, never the XVF3800 stream on :8765.
