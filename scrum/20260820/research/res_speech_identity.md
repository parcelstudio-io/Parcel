# Research report: Speech identity + acoustic robustness for F1 ("only the owner commands")

## Key works / systems

**1. XMOS XVF3800 host-control interface (Seeed reSpeaker firmware, 2024–25)** — The array Parcel already owns exposes exactly the metadata F1 needs, over a control channel that is *separate* from the audio stream. `xvf_host` (or the pure-Python `xvf_host.py`, all platforms) talks to USB control interface 3 (VID 10374 / PID 26) and supports: `AEC_AZIMUTH_VALUES` — read-only, returns four azimuths (focused beam 1, focused beam 2, free-running beam, auto-selected beam) in radians and degrees; the auto-selected beam is what drives the LED DoA ring. `AEC_SPENERGY_VALUES` — per-beam speech energy, i.e. hardware VAD with direction attached. Seeed's wiki also documents `DOA_VALUE` in the Python tooling and two USB firmware variants: 2-channel (ch0 Conference, ch1 ASR) and 6-channel (ch0–1 processed + ch2–5 raw per-mic), both 16 kHz/32-bit. *Why it matters:* DoA + speech-energy gating is essentially free — no model, no GPU, no added audio-path latency — and the TV is at a fixed bearing while the owner moves. *Implication:* poll `AEC_AZIMUTH_VALUES`/`AEC_SPENERGY_VALUES` at ~10–20 Hz from a sidecar process and stamp every gateway turn with (azimuth, energy). Docs don't state polling latency, and nothing confirms manual beam pinning — both need a bench test, done carefully since the live stack on :8765 owns the audio stream (control reads *should* be independent of the ISO audio endpoints, but verify; a firmware swap to 6-channel definitely disturbs it). Sources: [host_control README](https://github.com/respeaker/reSpeaker_XVF3800_USB_4MIC_ARRAY/blob/master/host_control/README.md), [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/respeaker_xvf3800_introduction/), [repo](https://github.com/respeaker/reSpeaker_XVF3800_USB_4MIC_ARRAY).

**2. ECAPA-TDNN (Desplanques et al., Interspeech 2020; SpeechBrain implementation)** — The de-facto open speaker-verification embedding (successor to Snyder's x-vectors, ICASSP 2018). VoxCeleb1 EER ~0.87% on full utterances; the crucial numbers for Parcel are duration-conditioned: roughly **~1% full, ~1.8% @5s, ~4% @2s, ~8–13% @1s**, degrading further in far-field/reverb (2–3x is a fair planning factor). ~20M params; a 2–6s utterance embeds in ~100–300 ms on a modern x86 CPU — fine to run per-turn next to the Gemma/Qwen boxes. Lite variants (ECAPA-TDNNLite, ~11.6M FLOPS, 3.07% EER; MFA-TDNN Lite) exist if per-frame streaming scores are wanted. *Implication:* score the whole turn utterance (Parcel's corpus queries are 2–6s — the sweet spot where EER is 2–4%, not the 1s disaster zone), cosine vs enrolled centroid, threshold calibrated in-situ. Sources: [multi-resolution short-segment ECAPA (IEEE)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10096839/), [duration-conditioned EERs / overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/ecapa-tdnn), [asymmetric enroll-verify](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04438), [MFA-TDNN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01624), [MR-RawNet duration study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07103), [short-utterance compensation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.10884).

**3. Resemblyzer / GE2E d-vectors (Wan et al. 2018)** — 256-dim d-vector encoder, trivially fast on CPU, ~3 lines of Python. Materially weaker than ECAPA (roughly 4–6% EER-class on VoxCeleb-style tests, worse on short far-field turns). *Implication:* fine for a day-one prototype of the gate plumbing; not the model to ship. The SpeechBrain ECAPA checkpoint costs nothing extra to use instead.

**4. Picovoice Eagle (2023)** — Commercial on-device speaker recognition: explicit enroll-then-recognize API, streaming per-frame similarity scores, 4.5 MB model, vendor-claimed 0.18% EER vs SpeechBrain 0.49% and pyannote 0.70% on their benchmark (vendor numbers; treat as directional). Enrollment API gives *progressive feedback* ("percentage complete, keep talking") — the best enrollment UX pattern in this list. *Implication:* even if Parcel stays open-source, copy Eagle's enrollment ergonomics: accumulate seconds-of-speech with a live progress signal, not "say these 3 phrases and pray." Sources: [Eagle repo](https://github.com/Picovoice/eagle), [product page](https://picovoice.ai/products/voice/speaker-recognition/).

**5. openWakeWord (dscripka, 2023) — especially its custom verifier models** — Open wake-word framework (15–20 models real-time on one Pi 3 core; target <0.5 false accepts/hr, <5% false rejects). The under-appreciated feature: an optional **custom verifier** — a tiny logistic-regression-on-features second stage trained from ~10s of the target speaker's speech plus recorded false-activation audio, which fires only after the wake model triggers and rejects non-target speakers. *Implication:* this is the exact "wake + identity" cascade pattern, pre-built, and its training recipe ("use previously collected false activations") maps directly onto Parcel's seeded-defect discipline — the recorded MBC-news hijack audio *is* verifier training data. Sources: [repo](https://github.com/dscripka/openWakeWord), [custom verifier docs](https://github.com/ykeselman/dscripka-openWakeWord/blob/main/docs/custom_verifier_models.md).

**6. Porcupine (Picovoice)** — Commercial wake word: 97%+ detection at <1 false alarm/10 hrs in noise, 0.6% CPU on a Pi 5, custom phrases without training data. *Implication:* the benchmark-grade option if a wake word enters the design at all; but for a companion robot a mandatory wake word is a persona tax — better held as a *fallback* modality (re-arm after rejections, and as an alternate e-stop trigger). Sources: [benchmark](https://picovoice.ai/blog/benchmarking-a-wake-word-detection-engine/), [product](https://picovoice.ai/products/voice/wake-word/).

**7. Personal VAD (Ding, Wang et al., 2019/Odyssey 2020; Personal VAD 2.0, 2022)** — Google's frame-level three-class detector (non-speech / target speech / non-target speech), conditioned on the enrolled speaker embedding, built to gate a streaming ASR so it "only triggers for the target user … where a keyword detector is unpreferable." That sentence is Parcel's F1 verbatim. *Implication:* the architectural blueprint for the end state — identity decided *before* audio is committed to the hosted model, not after. Parcel doesn't need to train one; DoA-gate + per-turn embedding approximates it, but the framing (gate the ASR input, three-way output) is the right mental model, and rejecting TV turns before commit also saves GPT-Realtime dollars — F1 has a cost dimension. Sources: [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04284), [Google page](https://google.github.io/speaker-id/publications/PersonalVAD/), [PVAD 2.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03793).

**8. VoiceFilter-Lite (Wang et al., Interspeech 2020)** — Streaming on-device *target-speaker separation*: strips overlapping non-target speech from the input feature stream, 8-bit quantized, asymmetric loss so it never hurts clean-speech ASR. *Implication:* the answer to the hardest F1 sub-case — owner and TV talking *simultaneously* (gating alone must reject or accept the whole turn; separation lets the owner's words through). Out of scope for v1; the correct citation for the roadmap. Sources: [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.04323), [Google page](https://google.github.io/speaker-id/publications/VoiceFilter-Lite/).

**9. Amazon's media wake suppression (Amazon Science, 2018–20; US patent 11,922,939)** — How Alexa survives its own Super Bowl ads: audio **fingerprinting** of known media (local subset on device, rest in cloud; wake events matching ≥2 other customers' simultaneous audio get auto-classified as media), claimed 80–90% suppression of TV-originated wakes. Note carefully what it is *not*: it is not AEC. **AEC only cancels audio the device itself plays (the loopback reference); the room TV is invisible to the XVF3800's AEC.** *Implication:* the fingerprint trick needs a fleet and doesn't transfer; the transferable lessons are (a) media immunity is a dedicated subsystem, not a side effect of echo cancellation, and (b) for a single home, the fixed-bearing TV plus speaker-ID is the poor-man's equivalent. Sources: [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/why-alexa-wont-wake-up-when-she-hears-her-name-in-amazons-super-bowl-ad), [AFTVnews analysis](https://www.aftvnews.com/amazon-uses-audio-fingerprints-to-prevent-mentions-of-alexa-in-broadcast-media-from-triggering-devices/), [patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11922939).

**10. Device-directed utterance detection (Amazon: Mallidi et al. 2018; Streaming ResLSTM 2020; implicit-AEC 2021)** — Classifiers that decide "was that addressed to *me*?" using acoustic features (energy, pitch, speaking rate, duration) fused with ASR-decoder features; powers Alexa Follow-Up Mode's wake-word-free turns. *Implication:* Parcel already holds both feature families per turn (audio + hosted transcript) and has a local Gemma reasoner idle at :8080 — a cheap second-line "directedness" check on the transcript ("MBC 뉴스 이덕영입니다" is self-evidently not a command) would have caught F1 even after the acoustic gate missed. Defense in depth, near-zero cost. Sources: [DDD 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.02504), [ResLSTM 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.09245), [implicit AEC 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10639), [natural turn-taking](https://www.amazon.science/blog/change-to-alexa-wake-word-process-adds-natural-turn-taking).

**11. diart / pyannote streaming diarization (Coria et al. 2021; pyannoteAI "Live-1" 2025)** — Online overlap-aware diarization on a 500 ms rolling buffer; commercial successor claims sub-300 ms speaker attribution. *Implication:* the ceiling option for two-speaker home scenes (owner + guest, owner + TV). Overkill for single-owner v1 — per-turn verification against one centroid is strictly simpler and more accurate for the "is it the owner?" question — but the right tool the day a second enrolled human appears. Sources: [diart](https://github.com/juanmc2005/diart), [latency evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.04293), [Live-1](https://www.pyannote.ai/blog/introducing-live-1-streaming-diarization).

**12. Google Voice Match enrollment UX (2020 revision)** — Production single-user-ish enrollment: four full spoken phrases (revised upward from bare "Hey Google" repetitions specifically to boost accuracy), up to six enrolled users, and — critically — Google *documents the failure mode* ("a voice similar to yours may be able to access your personal results"). *Implication:* honest-enrollment precedent — production systems pair few-utterance enrollment with an explicit accuracy caveat rather than pretending biometric certainty. Sources: [9to5Google on the 4-phrase revision](https://9to5google.com/2020/04/23/hey-google-voice-match/), [Google support](https://support.google.com/assistant/answer/9071681?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid).

## The cheapest reliable stack (focus-question answers)

**Option A — DoA + speech-energy gate alone.** Cost: one sidecar poller, zero models. Added turn latency: ~0 ms (runs parallel to capture). Catches: the *stationary* TV (fixed azimuth ±~15°) whenever the owner isn't co-linear with it. Fails: owner standing in the TV's bearing; TV moved; radio in another corner; any non-owner human. Verdict: necessary, not sufficient — but it alone would likely have stopped both ledgered MBC events.

**Option B — DoA + per-turn speaker embedding (recommended v1).** ECAPA (or Eagle) on the turn's audio vs owner centroid, fused with the azimuth stamp: `accept if (embedding ≥ τ_hi) or (embedding ≥ τ_lo and azimuth ∉ TV-zone and spenergy pattern says near-field)`. Added latency: ~100–300 ms if the gate must pass before commit; **~0 ms in the accept path if gating the disposition instead** — let audio stream to GPT Realtime, and on identity-fail cancel the response/suppress the tool disposition and strike the ledger row (Parcel's local-disposes architecture makes this unusually natural: identity becomes one more input to the deterministic disposer, like the single-beat tool-turn gate). Expected accuracy at 2–6s utterances, far-field: ~2–6% single-trial EER, but the two error directions are asymmetric and tunable — and TV audio specifically is doubly disadvantaged (wrong bearing *and* wrong voice, often wrong language).

**Option C — wake word + embedding (openWakeWord + custom verifier, or Porcupine + Eagle).** Highest precision, worst persona: every turn costs a wake phrase (~0.5–1 s of human time, negligible compute). Verdict: not the default lane for a companion robot; deploy as (a) fallback re-arm after N consecutive identity rejections, (b) a *second, independent* spoken e-stop trigger — which bears on F6.

**Safety asymmetry (do not skip):** the e-stop must be *exempt* from identity gating — "Die Stop" from any human voice, any direction, must latch. Identity gates actuation and conversation, never the stop path. Otherwise the F1 fix manufactures a new S-class defect.

**Honest single-owner enrollment:** Parcel already possesses a better enrollment set than any wizard would collect — `voice_corpus_v1`'s 52 owner WAVs (~3–4 min, same mic, same room, same processed channel). Centroid from those; then an Eagle-style progressive top-up during normal use (accepted high-confidence turns refresh the centroid). Honesty requirements: calibrate τ with *in-situ impostors* — replay public-corpus speech and the recorded MBC clip through the actual TV at the actual bearing (this also matches the TV's loudspeaker channel, which lab EERs never see); report the measured operating point (e.g. "rejects ~99% of TV speech, falsely rejects ~3% of your turns, one identity challenge per ~N turns") instead of claiming biometric identity; and say plainly that it is *recognition, not authentication* — a similar voice or a recording of the owner will pass (Google documents the same caveat; anti-spoofing/ASVspoof is out of scope for a companion).

## Design pressure on existing eval assets

**What's right:** owner_session_1's F1 evidence is unusually good — two ledgered TV rows with exact text and a barge-in causal chain, i.e. a ready-made regression fixture. voice_corpus_v1's record.sh captures the *processed* mono 16 kHz channel — exactly the domain the live stack hears, so embeddings prototyped on it transfer (a subtle thing many teams get wrong by prototyping on close-talk audio). 6-second fixed windows sit in the 2–6s band where verification EER is workable. Seeded-defect discipline maps 1:1 onto the openWakeWord verifier recipe (train on your own false accepts). The AutoRater both-orders/punt machinery generalizes to identity verdicts.

**What's wrong/missing:**
- **The corpus is 100% positive.** 52 should-command owner utterances, zero should-NOT-command audio. An identity gate evaluated on it can score perfectly by accepting everything. Needed: a negative corpus — TV/radio replayed through the room's actual TV at its actual bearing (news, dramas, ads; include Korean news specifically), a second human voice, owner-voice-from-TV-direction, and owner+TV overlap. Without it, F1 "fixes" are unfalsifiable.
- **No DoA ground truth is being recorded.** record.sh captures audio only; the control-channel metadata (`AEC_AZIMUTH_VALUES`, `AEC_SPENERGY_VALUES`) is not logged, so no recorded scenario can replay-test a DoA gate. Cheap fix: log the poller stream alongside every future capture, and stamp each ledger row with (azimuth, spenergy) at transcription time.
- **The eval model needs identity as a scored dimension** with two asymmetric metrics — Owner False Reject Rate (persona/latency cost) and Non-owner Command Acceptance Rate (safety/cost incident, weight it like a nav-safety baseline, frozen once green) — plus a third: *e-stop acceptance must remain 100% regardless of identity verdict* (ties into F6).
- **Latency/cost columns:** each gate variant (A/B/C above) should be measured as added-ms-per-turn and $-saved-per-rejected-turn in the live_run scoring, since rejected TV turns directly cut hosted-session spend.

**Pitfalls:** (1) Believing AEC covers the TV — it cancels only the robot's own playback reference; the TV needs DoA/identity, full stop. (2) Threshold calibration on close-talk or lab corpora — far-field + loudspeaker playback shifts scores; calibrate through the real TV. (3) Gating pre-commit and adding 300 ms to every turn when post-hoc disposition-cancel achieves the same safety at zero accept-path latency. (4) 1-second utterances ("Stop", "Wave") — EER ~8–13% there; short turns need the DoA prior and recent-identity hysteresis (a sticky "owner present at bearing θ" state), not a fresh embedding verdict. (5) Identity-gating the e-stop. (6) Disturbing the live array: control-interface polling is probably safe but unproven, and the 6-channel/raw firmware swap is definitely a stack outage — schedule it, don't drift into it. (7) Enrollment drift: a one-shot centroid ages (colds, distance, room changes); without a refresh policy the false-reject rate silently climbs, which for a companion robot reads as the robot "ignoring" its owner — a worse persona failure than the TV bug.
