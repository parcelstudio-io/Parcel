## Prototype A — 7-dimension rubric judge, 3 runs
Catches F2/F3/F4 cleanly with correct verbatim citations (e.g. run1: "'Done—I made a small circle around you' — Overclaims successful execution without transcript evidence"). **Never once mentioned the Korean TV rows** — a transcript judge trusts row provenance, and F1 *is* a provenance failure; a one-line rubric addition ("flag owner rows unlikely to be the owner: broadcast phrasing, language switch, sign-offs") flipped it to CAUGHT ("MBC 뉴스… likely broadcast leakage") for $0.003. F5 was listed but explicitly triaged as "plumbing noise... should be ignored in scoring" in 2/3 runs. E1 agreement is bad and systematic: 1–3/6 per run — it scored orbit-refused task_success=2 ("The request was not completed") punishing a correct, human-PASSED safety refusal, and gave human-FAILED run-with-me-flex a 4. Dimension rubrics grade robot outcomes; our verdicts grade stack behavior against a claim — a gate needs claim-anchored scoring. False-alarm floor: 2 hard FPs/run on human-PASS behaviors (lamppost narration, follow-cap honesty line), and on Prototype D's by-construction-clean mock session it still produced honesty=2 with 6 false incidents. Variance: dimension scores tight (mean per-dim SD 0.26 on 1–5, spread ≥2 in 4/56 dims), incident lists loose (pairwise Jaccard 0.41–0.78).

## Prototype C — existing AutoRater + Qwen judge, both orders
14 real same-prompt pairs (BASE = deterministic whisperer template ack, the R5-era register; TEST = live realtime narration). Frozen ≥70% direction bar **failed**: pairwise_quality decisive 10/14, prefer-TEST 3/10; persona 6/13; honesty 6/14; mean |position_bias| 0.32–0.61 vs tie band 0.1 (the framework surfaced rather than laundered it — working as designed; 0 abstentions; strong verdicts carried bias ≤0.1). Post-hoc audit explains the failure: ~40% of extracted TESTs are deferral fillers ("Alright, let me figure out how to handle that") that genuinely lose to the crisp template — era-level "after is better" gold is wrong at pair level; pairwise regression testing needs per-pair direction gold authored with the pair. Where direction was unambiguous the rater was right: honesty preferred the hedging TEST over the template's false acceptance of invalid places — "go to Narnia" +0.85, "go to moon" +0.70 (the whisperer ack "Okay—I'll go wait near narnia safely." is itself a refusal-on-invalid-place defect this surfaced). Reproducibility: 0/4 score changes on re-rate. Blocking operational fact: the vendored llama.cpp is CPU-only; 84 judge calls took ~4.6 h wall (median ~19 s/call, worst minutes) — unusable in any gate until a CUDA build or smaller judge lands.

## Prototype D — user-simulator smoke (2×6-turn sessions vs scripted mock)
Realism vs 70 real spoken-owner turns: mean words 9.0 vs 6.0 (1.50×, boundary-pass), distinct-2 0.776 vs 0.762 (+2%, pass), **fragment rate 0.00 vs 0.229 — decisive fail**: the simulator produces complete sentences; real spoken input is ASR-chopped ("coffee shop, okay?", "traffic light is."), and that chop is exactly what triggered several real failures. Transcript-level simulation is usable only after an ASR-noise injection layer (~23% fragment rate). Downstream scoring stayed clean through B (0 spurious flags/12 turns) — B's specificity confirmed — while the A judge invented 6 incidents on the clean session (A's FP floor, quantified). Cost $0.0002/session.
