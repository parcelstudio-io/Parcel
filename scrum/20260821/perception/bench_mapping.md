## Verdict: the geometry works, the semantics do not. QUALIFIED GO on the representation, NO-GO on text retrieval and on abstention.

All artifacts: `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-jaewoo-jang-Desktop-Projects-Parcel/799cb356-4cb4-445b-a784-306b6c6fd4a6/scratchpad/perception/bench-semmap/` — `PREREGISTRATION.md` (written before the run, with a disclosed deviations section), `render_frames.py`, `build_map.py`, `eval_map.py`, `diagnose.py`, `abstain_and_bias.py`, `out/*.json`.

**Setup.** 120 RGB-D frames rendered offscreen (EGL/NVIDIA) from a 32.0 m sidewalk-and-crossing patrol of `city_block.xml` (120 geoms), D455 nominal intrinsics at half res (640×360, fx=fy=322), Go2 mount from `camera_channel/d455.py` (0.35 m, 12° up), depth clipped to the real D455 band 0.4–6.0 m with out-of-band samples dropped. Mean valid-depth fraction 0.525. Camera pose taken as known — this bench tests semantics, not SLAM. Two arms: **A** = MuJoCo segmentation as an oracle detector (geom names never used), **B** = OWLv2-base-patch16-ensemble open-vocab (20-term vocabulary) + SigLIP2-base-patch16-224 crop embeddings, both fp16 on the RTX 5000 Ada, SigLIP2 batch 64, OWLv2 batch 1.

**Cost** (denominator 120 frames): render 3.3 ms/f; Arm B detect 121.9 ms/f, embed 8.7 ms/f, fuse 0.24 ms/f; peak VRAM 1.19 GB (Arm A 0.92 GB) — never above 1.2 GB of the 32.7 GB card, owner's 1.1 GB untouched. Compact map (centroids+768-d embeddings+evidence): **108.7 KiB for 36 places** (Arm A: 317.1 KiB / 105). Cost is a non-issue.

**Pre-registered scoring said GO. The added null control says that was wrong.** Arm B hit 7/8 at NAV tolerance, 3/8 at the repo's 0.30 m `RECOGNITION_LOCALIZATION_BUDGET_M`, median error 0.55 m. But re-scattering the same number of entries uniformly over the mapped area (500 draws/query) shows only **2 of 8 queries beat chance**: lamppost (0.01 m, p=0.002) and bench (0.23 m, p=0.036). Sidewalk and crosswalk score 0.00 m in the *random* map too (p=1.00, p=0.52) — those regions are large enough that the pre-registered containment metric is uninformative, a flaw in my own metric that I'm disclosing rather than patching. Grass (p=0.124), tree (p=0.132), door (p=0.644) are indistinguishable from random.

**The failure is retrieval, not fusion.** Where the detector fires correctly, back-projection is superb: the lamppost localizes to **1–3 cm**. SigLIP2 text→place cosine over the whole map spans only **0.060–0.135**, with top-vs-runner-up margins of 0.0004–0.01 — near-chance ranking. Arm A actually *lost* to Arm B (6/8 vs 7/8) because per-geom oracle segments are per-primitive (the bench is 4 separate geoms), so "perfect segmentation" yields worse object crops than OWLv2's boxes. Oracle segmentation bounds fusion, not semantics.

**Abstention fails outright — this is the safety-critical finding.** With 8 absent queries instead of the 1 I pre-registered, **no threshold separates present from absent**. "The parking garage" scores 0.107, above 5 of 8 *correct* answers. Corpus row 10 "Narnia" scores 0.073 and would send the robot to (−3.19, 4.10); row 11 "my office" to (−3.19, 4.10). Rejecting all absent queries costs 5/8 of the correct ones. SigLIP2's own calibrated probability for *correct* answers is 0.00014–0.163, so a p=0.5 gate rejects everything. Today rows 10–13 refuse correctly because the chain checks a closed label set; delete the labeled world and that refusal capability disappears. Note the detector itself is innocent — OWLv2 never once fired "a coffee shop" in 120 frames. The hallucination is entirely in embedding-space retrieval.

**Detector domain gap, concretely** (512 detections / 120 frames): most common class is "a bollard" ×157 in a scene containing **one** bollard — it fires on lampposts, tree trunks, and pedestrian capsules. Never fires at all: door, planter, coffee shop, car, bicycle, storefront, street light. **People: 10/107 frames** in which a person or cyclist occupies ≥400 px = **9.3% recall**. Person-yield cannot ride on this.

**Two findings transfer to real hardware; one does not.**
- *Transfers — facade bias, and the answer key is wrong.* Building entries land 1–3 cm from the **visible facade** and 1.2–1.7 m from the geom centre, 6/6 in Arm A, 5/6 in Arm B. That is correct sensor behavior — a depth camera sees surfaces, never centroids. `scene_truth.json`'s centre+radius convention is unmeasurable by any RGB-D sensor. "Go to the building" should target the facade; the cutover needs a surface-based ground-truth convention or every building query will score as a failure while being right.
- *Transfers — abstention.* Cosine-ranked retrieval with no calibrated absolute score cannot say "I don't know," and this is architectural, not a sim artifact.
- *Does NOT transfer — the detector numbers.* The imagery is flat-shaded untextured primitives, pedestrians are colored capsules with sphere heads, and at 0.35 m eye height single surfaces fill whole frames (see `out/sample_grid.png`). OWLv2's 9.3% person recall is weak evidence about real D455 imagery. **This sim cannot answer the perception-generalization question** — it has no texture, no signage, and no coffee shop, so it cannot exercise the OCR/storefront path the 9 skipped gate tests cover.

**Recommendation.** Don't cut over against this scene. Three things, in order: (1) fix the ground-truth convention to surface-based before measuring anything else, or you will mis-grade a working pipeline; (2) get a calibrated abstention mechanism — detector-label agreement plus evidence count is a candidate, since OWLv2's label head *did* correctly abstain on "coffee shop" where embedding cosine did not; (3) re-run this bench on textured imagery before trusting any detector number. The fusion and spatial representation are sound and cheap (108 KiB, 130 ms/frame) — I'd keep them.
